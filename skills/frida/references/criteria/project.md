# (P) Project: frida criteria

One entry per section: what gets decided here, where teams close early, and what I ask. These
sharpen the three lenses in `cross-cutting.md`; they do not replace them. "Where teams close
early" is my note to myself, not a line I deliver.

Every question names an axis. None of them names what the answer should be. If the team could
paste my words into their document as a requirement, I wrote the question wrong.

## (P.1) Roles and personnel

`parts/project/P1.adoc` · milestone 3

**What gets decided here.** How the work is divided, and what that division assumes about who is available.

**Where teams close early.** The split mirrors existing friendships and existing skills, and nobody wrote down who does the work that nobody wants.

- Why this division? Did somebody choose it, or did it just settle?
- Who does the work nobody wants, and is that written anywhere?
- What happens when one person is unavailable for two weeks?
- Which role exists because somebody wanted it rather than because the project needs it?

## (P.2) Imposed technical choices

`parts/project/P2.adoc` · milestone 3

**What gets decided here.** What the team agrees to treat as imposed rather than chosen.

**Where teams close early.** Preferences get relabelled as impositions, because an imposition needs no justification and a preference does.

- Who imposed this, and did you ask them why?
- Which of these would you have chosen anyway?
- What would you use if nothing at all were imposed?
- Which of these have you not actually tested? Some impositions are softer than they look.

## (P.3) Schedule and milestones

`parts/project/P3.adoc` · milestone 3

**What gets decided here.** The shape of the plan: what happens in what order, and what is assumed to be sequential.

**Where teams close early.** A straight line from now to the deadline, with everything in the order it was thought of and no slack anywhere in particular.

- What forced this order? Which parts are genuinely sequential?
- What could happen in parallel that you have put in sequence?
- Which date here has slack, and which has none?
- What is the plan if the first milestone slips by two weeks?

## (P.4) Tasks and deliverables

`parts/project/P4.adoc` · milestone 3

**What gets decided here.** What counts as one unit of work, and what counts as done.

**Where teams close early.** Tasks are sized to fit the boxes in the schedule rather than the work, so the awkward ones get hidden inside the big ones.

- Who decided a task was this size? What is inside the largest one?
- Which deliverable would you drop first, and what would that cost?
- What work is going to happen that has no task here at all?
- Which of these does nobody want, and what is your plan for that?

## (P.5) Required technology elements

`parts/project/P5.adoc` · milestone 3

**What gets decided here.** What the project agrees to depend on.

**Where teams close early.** Familiar tools, chosen because somebody on the team already knew them, with no alternative ever written down.

- Why this one? What else could serve, and did anybody look?
- What happens if it is unavailable in March?
- Which of these did you choose, and which did you inherit?
- What are you depending on that is not listed because it is invisible to you?

## (P.6) Risk and mitigation analysis

`parts/project/P6.adoc` · milestone 1

**What gets decided here.** Which futures are worth planning for, and which are treated as not worth naming.

**Where teams close early.** Generic risks that would apply to any project in the course, because the specific ones are uncomfortable to write next to your own names.

- What is the risk you have not written down because it is awkward?
- Which of these would you not recover from?
- What would somebody outside your team say your biggest risk is?
- From milestone 3, on the threats: who benefits from attacking this, and what do they walk away with?

## (P.7) Requirements process and report

`parts/project/P7.adoc` · milestone 1

**What gets decided here.** How requirements were gathered, who was asked, and what counts as having asked.

**Where teams close early.** The process that was convenient. Everyone available was consulted and everyone else became out of scope without anybody deciding that.

- Who did you not ask, and why not?
- What would a different way of eliciting have surfaced that yours did not?
- What surprised you? If nothing did, did you ask anything you did not already know the answer to?
- What would you do differently if you started this again on Monday?
