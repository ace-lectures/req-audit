# (G) Goals: frida criteria

One entry per section: what gets decided here, where teams close early, and what I ask. These
sharpen the three lenses in `cross-cutting.md`; they do not replace them. "Where teams close
early" is my note to myself, not a line I deliver.

Every question names an axis. None of them names what the answer should be. If the team could
paste my words into their document as a requirement, I wrote the question wrong.

## (G.1) Context and Overall Objectives

`parts/goals/G1.adoc` · milestone 1

**What gets decided here.** Which context counts as relevant, where the edge of the organisation is drawn, and what the problem gets called.

**Where teams close early.** The first framing sticks, usually whoever pitched the project. The context gets written as though it were the only possible reading of the situation.

- Whose account of this situation is it? Who would describe the same context differently?
- What did you decide the problem is? What else could it have been called?
- Which of these objectives were handed to you, and which did you pick?
- If this project simply did not happen, what would the organisation do instead?

## (G.2) Current situation

`parts/goals/G2.adoc` · milestone 1

**What gets decided here.** What counts as current, whose experience of it gets written down, and where the process is treated as starting and stopping.

**Where teams close early.** The situation is described from one role's point of view, usually the one person the team actually spoke to.

- Whose day does this describe? Who else is in this process, and does it look like this to them?
- Where does this process start? What happens just before that, and did you decide it was out of scope or simply not look?
- What is working today that this project might break?
- Who likes the current situation? Somebody usually does.

## (G.3) Expected Benefits

`parts/goals/G3.adoc` · milestone 1

**What gets decided here.** Who benefits, over what timescale, and what gets to count as a benefit at all.

**Where teams close early.** Benefits accrue to whoever commissioned the project and to nobody else, and they are assumed to arrive the day it ships.

- Who gets these benefits, and who does the work that produces them?
- On what timescale? What does this look like in week one, and in year three?
- What would a benefit to somebody outside the organisation look like here?
- Which of these would still be worth having if the system did half of what you plan?

## (G.4) Functionality overview

`parts/goals/G4.adoc` · milestone 1

**What gets decided here.** Which functions count as principal, and how they get grouped.

**Where teams close early.** The list is the first brainstorm, in the order people thought of things, and the grouping is whatever the whiteboard happened to look like.

- Why are these the principal ones? Which nearly made the list?
- What did you leave out of this overview? Was that a decision or an oversight?
- Group these a different way. Does another grouping suggest a function you do not have?
- If the system did only the first one, would it be worth building?

## (G.5) High-level usage scenarios

`parts/goals/G5.adoc` · milestone 2

**What gets decided here.** Which paths count as main, who the user in them is, and where a scenario is allowed to start and stop.

**Where teams close early.** One imagined typical user doing the thing correctly. The scenario starts when they arrive and ends when they leave, so everything around it is invisible.

- Who is the user in these? What if they were brand new, in a hurry, hostile, or doing this for the hundredth time today?
- Where does this scenario start? What happened before that, and who did it?
- Which of your G.7 categories never appears in any scenario at all?
- What is the scenario where somebody uses this to do something you did not intend?

## (G.6) Limitations and Exclusions

`parts/goals/G6.adoc` · milestone 2

**What gets decided here.** Where the edge of the system sits, and whether something excluded is refused or merely deferred.

**Where teams close early.** Exclusions are really ran-out-of-time, written in the language of scope. Nobody records who wanted the excluded thing.

- For each of these: out of scope forever, or just not this term? Which one does the text say?
- Who wanted the thing you excluded, and what did you tell them?
- What would have to change for one of these to come back into scope?
- What is in scope that you have never once questioned?

## (G.7) Stakeholders and requirements sources

`parts/goals/G7.adoc` · milestone 1

**What gets decided here.** Who counts as a stakeholder, and how coarse the categories are.

**Where teams close early.** The list is the people the team could reach rather than the people who matter, and a category gets treated as one group when it is really three.

- Which of these did you actually talk to, and which did you imagine?
- Who is affected and hard to reach? Hard to reach is not the same as not mattering.
- Split your biggest category in two. Do the halves want different things?
- Who would be harmed by this system working exactly as intended?
