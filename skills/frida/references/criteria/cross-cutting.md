# Cross-cutting criteria: frida

Three families of lens, and I reach for whichever bites. They apply to any section, whatever it is
about.

**How to use this file.** It is a bank, not a script. I do not walk it end to end, I do not run
every lens on every section, and I do not announce which one I am using. I pick the one or two
that open the most in the text in front of me.

**The discipline that governs all of it: axes, never values.** Every question below names a
dimension along which a decision was made. None of them names what the decision should be instead.

Naming the two ends of a generic dimension is still naming the dimension: once or continuously,
one actor or many, reversible or not. Those are properties any system has. Naming what *this*
system should do about them is the line, and I do not cross it.

The test, when I am unsure: could the team paste my words into their document as a requirement? If
yes, I have written the question wrong.

**No specimens anywhere.** Not in this file and not in a session: no well-formed requirement, no
example from another domain, no shape to fill in. The lists below are dimensions to ask about, not
options to choose from.

# Lens 1: decisions written as facts

The core one. A requirements document is a pile of choices with the choosing filed off. Every
sentence that reads as a fact about the world is a candidate.

## The unnamed decision

The tell: "the system will", "users need", "the process is", stated flatly, where "we decided" is
the honest verb.

- Is that a fact about the world, or something you decided? Who decided it, and when?
- What would have to be true for the opposite to be the right call?
- If a new team member asked why it is this way, what would you tell them, and is that written
  down anywhere?

## The second option

- What else was on the table when you settled this?
- If nothing was, what made the first idea sufficient?
- How long did this take to decide? The ones that took a minute are the ones worth reopening.

## The inherited framing

- Which earlier section forced this to look the way it does?
- If that section changed next week, would this one survive it?
- You are three milestones in. Which decision in here was made before you understood the problem?

## The cost of being right

- Suppose this works exactly as written. Who is worse off than they are today?
- What does this make harder that used to be easy?
- Who would read this section and disagree, and what would they say?

# Lens 2: structured moves

Deliberate manipulations. I apply one to the text and ask what happens, which is faster than
asking a team to be creative on demand.

## Inversion

- Write the opposite of this sentence. Is the opposite obviously wrong, or just unfamiliar?
- What if the system did nothing here at all? Who notices?
- What if this were somebody's job rather than the system's?

## Constraint removal

- Which constraint in E.3 is this shaped around? Take it away: what changes?
- If you had twice the time, would this be different? If not, why is it not different now?
- What are you not attempting because of something you assumed rather than something imposed?

## Changing who acts

- Who starts this? What if it were somebody else, or nobody, or the system on its own?
- Which of your stakeholder categories could do this instead?
- What if the person doing this were hostile, bored, or brand new?

## Changing the timescale

- What if this happened continuously instead of once, or once instead of continuously?
- What does this look like on day one with no data, and in year three with too much?
- What happens if it takes a hundred times longer than you assumed?

## Changing the scale

- Ten times as many. What is the first thing that breaks?
- One user, one item, one transaction. Is any of this still worth building?
- What if there were exactly zero of the thing this section assumes there are many of?

## The outside view

- How does anyone else solve this problem? Not a system: any organisation, any century.
- What would a team who had never met your stakeholders build from this section?
- Read it as a stranger. What would you assume that you have not been told?

# Lens 3: the people who are missing

Requirements are written by the people in the room about the people in the room.

## The absent party

- Who is affected by this and appears nowhere in G.7?
- Who is downstream of this: who receives what this produces, or cleans up after it?
- Which categories in G.7 never appear again after G.7?

## The unwilling participant

- Who has to use this and did not choose to?
- Who is measured by what this produces?
- Who would be relieved if this project were cancelled?

## The unwritten path

- How many of the interactions here end badly? Where is the one that does not work out?
- What does an interaction abandoned halfway through leave behind?
- Who uses this in a way you did not intend, and what do they get out of it?

## The single perspective

- How many kinds of person are in this section? If it is one, is that the world or the writing?
- Whose vocabulary is this written in? Would the people it describes recognise themselves?
- Which of your stakeholders would read this and say it is not about them at all?
