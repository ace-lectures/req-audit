# Using the reviewers

## Before you start

Open your **document repository** — the one with `index.adoc`, `metadata.adoc` and `parts/` in it
— in your agent. The personas read the AsciiDoc sources directly; there is nothing to upload,
paste, or build first.

Make sure `metadata.adoc` names your team and your current milestone. The persona will ask you to
confirm the milestone anyway, but a correct file saves a round trip.

## Picking a persona

| | Ask when you want |
|---|---|
| **bertrand** | To be argued with. Is this sentence precise? Could anyone verify it? Why is this here? Does it belong in this section? |
| **peggy** | To be widened. What else could this have been? Who did we not think about? What are we assuming without saying so? |

They are complementary, not sequential. A common pattern is **peggy first** while a section is
still soft, then **bertrand** once you have committed to a framing and need it to hold up.

Nothing stops you running both on the same section. They will disagree with each other
occasionally; that is a useful signal about where the section is genuinely undecided.

## What a session looks like

You invoke a persona, it reads `metadata.adoc`, and it asks which milestone and which section.
Then, one section at a time:

- it quotes lines from your section;
- it says what troubles it about them;
- it asks two or three questions;
- it stops and waits for you.

You answer. It follows up, or agrees and moves on. You decide when to go to the next section.

There is no report at the end. There is no score. The output of a session is what you decide to
change, and you make those changes yourself.

## What they will not do

**They do not write your document.** Not a section, not a paragraph, not one sentence, not an
example. If you ask, you will get a short refusal and a question back.

This is deliberate, and it is not negotiable by rephrasing the request. Every author on the
document signs an academic-integrity affirmation in `parts/integrity.adoc`; text generated for you
would breach it. The reviewers are built so that using them cannot put you in that position.

They also never edit your files. Everything happens in conversation; you make every change.

## Getting more out of it

- **Answer the questions out loud, in the session, before editing.** If you can answer a question
  clearly in chat but the answer is not in the document, you have found the real gap.
- **Bring a section you think is finished.** Sections you know are weak produce obvious findings.
- **Push back.** If you disagree with a reason, say so. The reason stands, and it usually belongs
  in the document — the template asks for requirements to be justified.
- **Don't run all 26 sections in one sitting.** Two or three sections is a productive session.
